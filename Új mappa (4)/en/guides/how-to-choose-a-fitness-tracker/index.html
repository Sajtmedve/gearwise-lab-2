How to choose a fitness tracker or smartwatch (2026 buying guide)

Updated: 6 November 2026

The right wearable is the one you will actually wear every day. For most people that means a light tracker with long battery life, unless you want apps, payments and notifications on your wrist; then a smartwatch is worth the extra charging.

Fitness tracker or smartwatch?

Fitness tracker Smartwatch
Main job Steps, heart rate, sleep, workouts All of that plus apps, notifications, payments
Typical battery life About a week or more Often 1–3 days (some sports watches much longer)
Size and comfort Slim, easy to sleep with Bigger screen, heavier
Price range Entry level Mid to premium

Rule of thumb: if sleep tracking matters to you, battery life and comfort matter more than any extra feature. A watch you take off at night cannot track your sleep.

The features that actually matter

1. Phone compatibility

Check this first. Some smartwatches only work fully with one phone platform (for example, Apple Watch requires an iPhone). Most trackers work with both Android and iOS.

2. Battery life

Manufacturers quote battery life with everything switched off. Always-on display, GPS workouts and blood-oxygen readings during sleep can cut it in half. Look for real-world numbers in user reviews.

3. GPS

Built-in GPS records your route and pace without your phone. If you only run or cycle with your phone in your pocket, "connected GPS" (using the phone's GPS) is enough and cheaper.

4. Heart rate and health readings

Nearly every device has an optical heart-rate sensor. It is good for resting heart rate and steady workouts, less precise for interval training. For serious training, a chest strap is still the most accurate option.

Wearables are not medical devices unless explicitly certified as such. Treat readings like sleep stages, stress or blood oxygen as trends, not diagnoses. If something worries you, talk to a doctor.

5. Subscriptions

Some brands put advanced insights (detailed sleep analysis, training readiness, coaching) behind a monthly subscription. Check what is free before you buy, and add the yearly cost to the price.

6. Water resistance

For swimming, look for at least 5 ATM (50 m) and a dedicated swim mode. "Splash resistant" is only for rain and hand washing.

Quick checklist before you buy

  • Does it fully support my phone?
  • Is the real-world battery life long enough to wear it at night?
  • Do I need built-in GPS, or is my phone enough?
  • Are the features I want free, or behind a subscription?
  • Is it comfortable enough to wear 24/7?

FAQ

Is a cheap tracker accurate enough? For steps, resting heart rate and sleep trends, entry-level trackers are usually good enough. Accuracy differences show up mainly in GPS and high-intensity workouts.

Can I use a smartwatch without a smartphone? Some models with LTE work independently for a while, but setup and most features still need a phone.
